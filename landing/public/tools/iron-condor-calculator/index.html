How to calculate an iron condor

An iron condor is a put credit spread below the stock and a call credit spread above it, opened together for one credit. At expiration the stock can only finish past one of the short strikes, so only one wing can lose, and the max loss is set by the wider wing.

Max profit      = total credit × 100 × contracts
Max loss        = (wider wing width - total credit) × 100 × contracts
Lower breakeven = short put strike - total credit
Upper breakeven = short call strike + total credit
Profit zone     = upper breakeven - lower breakeven
Return on risk  = max profit / max loss

Use the total credit for all four legs in every formula, not the credit of one side. That is what moves both breakevens out: the credit from the call spread also protects the put side, and the other way round.

Worked example

Hypothetical: a stock trades at $100. You buy the 90 put, sell the 95 put, sell the 105 call and buy the 110 call for a total credit of $1.80. Both wings are $5 wide.

  • Max profit: $1.80 × 100 = $180.00, kept if the stock finishes between $95 and $105.
  • Max loss: ($5.00 - $1.80) × 100 = $320.00, if it finishes below $90 or above $110.
  • Breakevens: $95 - $1.80 = $93.20 and $105 + $1.80 = $106.80, a profit zone $13.60 wide.
  • Return on risk: $180.00 / $320.00 = 56.3%.

Unequal wings

When one wing is wider, the loss on that side is larger and the broker usually holds the wider width. With a $10 put wing and a $5 call wing for the same $1.80, the put side can lose $820 and the call side $320. The calculator shows both under the figures, so you can see which side carries the risk.

What the calculator does not tell you

  • The odds. A wider profit zone and a higher return on risk pull against each other; the calculator shows both, but neither is a probability. The expected move calculator shows how far the market prices the stock to move.
  • Before expiration. A jump in implied volatility can lift the value of both spreads at once, so the condor can show a loss while the stock is still between the short strikes.
  • Fees and assignment. Four legs means four commissions each way where your broker charges them. A short leg that goes in the money can be assigned early.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the breakeven of an iron condor?

An iron condor has two breakevens. The lower one is the short put strike minus the total credit; the upper one is the short call strike plus the total credit. Use the credit for all four legs, not one side's.

What is the max loss on an iron condor?

The width of the wider wing minus the total credit, times 100 per contract. Only one side can finish in the money at expiration, so you never lose on both wings at once.

How much margin does an iron condor need?

Most brokers hold the width of the wider wing times 100 per condor. Some reduce that by the credit received, which makes it equal to the max loss. Your broker's figure is the one that applies.

Is a higher return on risk better on an iron condor?

Not by itself. Return on risk rises when the short strikes sit closer to the stock price, which also makes it more likely the stock finishes past one of them. The calculator does not estimate that likelihood.

What is the difference between an iron condor and an iron butterfly?

An iron butterfly sells the put and the call at the same strike, usually at the money, so the profit zone is narrower and the credit is larger. Enter the same strike for the short put and the short call and the calculator handles it.

Can an iron condor lose on both sides?

At expiration, no: the stock finishes in one place. Before expiration, a rise in implied volatility can raise the value of both spreads together, so the position can show a loss with the stock between the short strikes.
